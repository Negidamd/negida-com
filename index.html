Research Program

Cholinergic vulnerability and cognitive resilience in Lewy body disorders and Alzheimer’s disease

I develop scalable multimodal imaging biomarkers of cholinergic degeneration to predict cognitive outcomes and select patients for therapeutic trials in Parkinson’s disease, dementia with Lewy bodies, and Alzheimer’s disease.

  1. Problem

    Cholinergic degeneration contributes to cognitive impairment in all three diseases and is the rationale for cholinesterase inhibitors in dementia. Its extent and regional pattern vary between patients, and cognitive impairment does not follow a single pattern of cholinergic loss.

    New cholinergic therapies are in trials: muscarinic agonists (xanomeline–trospium; the M1 agonist NSC001), Ch4 deep brain stimulation, and regenerative tissue implanted into Ch4. Unlike amyloid and tau trials, the cholinergic pipeline lacks an enrichment biomarker that selects patients with cholinergic degeneration. Can imaging identify who is most likely to benefit?

  2. Target: Ch4 and its cortical connections

    Ch4 (the nucleus basalis of Meynert) supplies most cortical cholinergic input and degenerates in these diseases. I measure its structure, microstructure, and cortical connectivity to capture degeneration and preservation that volume alone does not reveal.

    ↓

    Cognitive resilience

    Some patients keep better cognition than their Ch4 degeneration would predict. I study whether regional cholinergic preservation and network connectivity explain this resilience.

  3. Approach

    • Multimodal MRI. Structural, diffusion, and functional imaging of Ch4 integrity and cortical connectivity.
    • Cholinergic PET benchmarking. Relating MRI signatures to regional cholinergic innervation.
    • Complementary EEG. Network dysfunction and cognitive fluctuations.
    • Subtyping and longitudinal modeling. Patient subgroups, deviations from normative trajectories, and prediction of cognitive decline and dementia.

  4. Applications

    Validated imaging biomarkers for patient stratification in cholinergic trials, with secondary goals of refining prognosis and testing whether baseline cholinergic status explains differences in treatment response.

    Longer term: assessing residual target integrity before Ch4 stimulation or restorative therapies.

Contributions

1#: In vivo imaging of cholinergic integrity

Ch4 is small and difficult to delineate with conventional MRI volumetry. Using probabilistic cytoarchitectonic maps, I quantified Ch4 integrity from standard MRI and identified a subtype of PD mild cognitive impairment (PD-MCI) defined by MRI evidence of Ch4 degeneration. I then added diffusion MRI, estimating Ch4 free water with the regularized bi-tensor model of Pasternak et al. (2009). In resting-state fMRI, Ch4 connectivity with the attention-related cingulo-opercular network mediated the relationship between Ch4 degeneration and cognition, a candidate circuit link to cognitive impairment.

2#: EEG Signature of Lewy Body Dementia

EEG is inexpensive, repeatable, and widely available, and cognitive fluctuations in Lewy body dementia are commonly attributed to cholinergic degeneration. In the NIH R21 AG077469 study, I analyzed resting-state EEG in patients with Lewy body disease and built a machine-learning model that detects cognitive fluctuations. Building on this work, I contributed pilot data and the multimodal Ch4 MRI pipeline to a funded mechanistic R01 on cholinergic degeneration and cognitive fluctuations, which currently supports my position.

3#: Modeling Disease Progression

Heterogeneity in progression also complicates disease-modifying trials. Using PPMI, I modeled ten-year progression milestones across clinical, pathological, and data-driven PD subtypes; diffuse-malignant PD progressed fastest. To identify these patients prospectively, I developed disease-duration–specific percentiles for the first five years after diagnosis, released an open-source subtype calculator, and characterized the genetic associations of these subtypes. This work converges with my imaging research: patients with PD-MCI and MRI evidence of Ch4 degeneration overlap with those classified as diffuse-malignant PD.

Featured work

Low Ch4 gray-matter density marks a more severe, faster-declining PD-MCI group

Using voxel-based morphometry and probabilistic Ch4 maps in 162 PPMI participants with PD-MCI, we identified 40 with low Ch4 density (below −1 SD). They had worse MDS-UPDRS, autonomic, and olfactory scores and reached cognitive milestones sooner (log-rank p = 0.002). The classifier is released as an open research tool.

Ch4 gray-matter density and free water are independently associated with cognition in PD (preprint)

We combined T1-weighted and free-water diffusion MRI in 92 participants (controls, PD-NC, PD-MCI, PDD). Ch4 gray-matter density was lowest and free water highest in PDD. Only these two Ch4 measures were independently associated with cognition, and together they separated PDD from PD without dementia (exploratory AUC 0.93).

medRxiv preprint, 2026 →

Ch4–cingulo-opercular connectivity links Ch4 degeneration to cognition

Resting-state fMRI extends the multimodal Ch4 signature. Ch4 connectivity with the attention-related cingulo-opercular network statistically mediated the relationship between Ch4 degeneration and cognition, supporting a candidate circuit link between Ch4 degeneration and cognitive impairment.

Slower resting EEG rhythms are associated with cognitive fluctuations in Lewy body disease

Eyes-closed resting EEG in 53 patients across the Lewy body spectrum. Patients with fluctuations had lower dominant frequency, dominant-alpha prevalence, and individual alpha peak frequency after adjusting for age, sex, and MoCA; an elastic-net model detected fluctuations with AUC 0.86. The topographic maps show mean dominant frequency at every electrode: about 6–7 Hz in patients with fluctuations versus about 8 Hz in those without.

Alzheimer's Research & Therapy, 2026 → CC BY 4.0

Diffuse-malignant PD progresses fastest; enrolling it halved required sample sizes in trial simulations

Over 10 years of PPMI follow-up, we compared clinical, pathological, and data-driven subtypes on milestones across six functional domains using Cox regression. Diffuse-malignant patients reached 63.0% of milestones (PIGD 55.6%, body-first 54.0%), and in trial simulations enrolling them cut the required sample size by about 50%.

npj Parkinson's Disease, 2026 → CC BY 4.0 · cropped

Year-specific percentiles enable real-time PD subtyping for trial enrollment

Percentile thresholds for motor, cognitive, RBD, and autonomic scores, set for each of the first 5 years after diagnosis (PPMI, N = 1,030), classify patients prospectively each year. Diffuse-malignant PD reached a first progression milestone fastest (HR 3.03, 95% CI 2.30–3.97, vs mild motor-predominant).

Mov Disord Clin Pract, 2026 → CC BY-NC-ND 4.0

LRRK2 and GBA1 map onto different PD subtypes

Variant frequencies were compared across four subtyping frameworks in 1,390 genotyped PPMI patients with FDR correction. LRRK2 variants were enriched in α‑synuclein SAA-negative PD (37.1% vs 10.2%) and GBA1 variants in diffuse-malignant PD (14.0% vs 5.9% in mild motor-predominant); the tremor/gait split showed no signal that survived correction.

TBI mortality across 29 countries does not follow a development gradient

This prospective cohort enrolled 2,165 patients with operative or nonoperative TBI at 100 hospitals. In inverse-probability-weighted mixed-effects models, mortality showed no dose-response with the Human Development Index; injury mechanism, demographics, and prehospital access drove the disparities. PD Subtyping Tool

Mov Disord Clin Pract, 2026 · npj Parkinson's Disease, 2026

Clinical, pathological, and data-driven subtyping of Parkinson's disease. Disease-duration–specific percentiles (PPMI, N = 1,030) classify patients as diffuse-malignant, intermediate, or mild motor-predominant for real-time trial stratification.

Neuroimaging Methods

Negida-DTI-ALPS

Diffusion MRI · Open Source

DTI-ALPS pipeline for perivascular (glymphatic) diffusivity that uses ANTs SyN and T1-based boundary-based registration to correct ROI misplacement under high head rotation. Validated against the reference alps.sh toolbox in 106 diffusion-MRI datasets (r = 0.96 for mean ALPS); MIT-licensed.

GitHub →

CNN-PD-3D

Deep Learning · Open Source

Convolutional neural network for automated Parkinson's Disease detection from structural 3D T1-weighted MRI. End-to-end pipeline from preprocessing to classification with interpretability visualization.

GitHub →

Vol-HD

Movement Disorders, 2024

Online calculator for predicting striatal volumes in Huntington's Disease patients using demographic and clinical variables, enabling pre-screening for clinical trials of intrastriatal gene therapies.

Teaching and Mentorship

Mentorship Track Record

I have mentored 50+ junior researchers to first-author publications in peer-reviewed journals, using a model built around staged project scoping, weekly writing accountability, and graduated independence, and have supervised graduate and undergraduate students in neuroimaging analysis, machine learning, and systematic reviews.

Named mentees: Hazem Ghaith, MD (Postdoctoral Scholar, Department of Neurological Surgery, Oregon Health & Science University) and Hussien Abdelgawad, MD (Research Scientist, University of Arizona; Assistant Research Professor, Mayo Clinic Arizona). Further mentees have first-authored peer-reviewed papers across academic medical centers in Europe and the Middle East.

Negida Research Academy

Founded in 2014, Negida Research Academy is a global virtual training platform in clinical research methods: study design, biostatistics, systematic reviews, meta-analysis, scientific writing, and grant preparation. It runs two structured cohort-based training camps each year, each training approximately 1,200 researchers over 8–12 weeks. Author of the Negida Handbook of Clinical Research.

35,000+ total trainees  ·  20+ countries  ·  2,500+ active members

Global Research Training

Most Academy trainees work in low- and middle-income countries in the Middle East, Africa, and South Asia. I developed the first neuroimaging analysis course in Arabic (MRI preprocessing, VBM, and fMRI with MATLAB/SPM12/CAT12), and in 2022 I provided free, specialized training to African medical students affected by the war in Ukraine; their work resulted in a peer-reviewed publication (PMID: 38087277).
